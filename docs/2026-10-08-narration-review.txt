River Hydrograph and Precipitation Hyetograph. 24 hours. River flow and rainfall over the last 24 hours, shown together on one chart. Rainfall totals from Environment Agency station 49149 measured every 15 minutes in millimetres are plotted on the left, while river flow rate a measure of volume per time measured in cubic metres per second is calculated and plotted on the right. Date & Time are shown along the bottom. This allows correlation between rainfall and the change of river level. It also helps show the response time of the river to rainfall and the speed at which the volume of water rises. The light blue line represents calculated river flow in cubic metres per second. Blue bars, when available, represent rainfall in millimetres. The two measurements use separate vertical axes.

Read aloud: Daily Range and Peak Levels

Daily Range and Peak Levels. This plot shows the relationship between daily water depth range maximum to minimum and the corresponding daily maximum water depth since the Flash Flood Observatory began operating. A clear upward trend is visible: days with larger water depth ranges also reach higher maximum water depths. Most days cluster at low values shown at the bottom left, reflecting stable, non-event conditions, while a small number of events form a separate group shown at the top right with both high ranges and high maximum depths. This pattern supports the classification of Boscastle as a flashy catchment where steep slopes and slow-draining geology cause rapid response even from small, localised events. Each plotted point represents one completed day. The horizontal axis shows its water depth range and the vertical axis shows its maximum water depth, both in metres.

Read aloud: Daily maximum water level (% of average)

River Levels. Daily maximum water level, shown as a percentage of the average water depth. This heatmap shows the maximum daily water depth as a percentage of the average water depth, recorded since the Flash Flood Observatory deployment, in November 2025 (currently equal to 0.172 metres). Each cell represents a specific day of the week and month within the selected hydrological year, which is defined from the 1st of October to the 30th of September, allowing patterns in river behaviour to be compared without the heatmap growing indefinitely. The colours indicate how each day's maximum level compares to the average: days close to the average appear as white squares, wetter-than-average days appear as blue squares or purple squares, and drier-than-average days appear as brown squares. This makes it easy to identify periods of typical flow, sustained dry spells, and clusters of high-water days associated with storm events or rapid catchment response. A value such as 30 percent means the river reached 30 percent of the average water depth. 0.3 times 0.172 metres equals 0.0516 metres, and is represented with a brown square. The highest band, above 450 percent, represents days above 450 percent of the average water depth. 4.5 times 0.172 metres equals 0.7740 metres, and is represented with a purple square. Each coloured square represents a completed day's maximum water depth as a percentage of the overall average since deployment, across all water years. A grey square with a diagonal line means no data. Blank space to the right is reserved for the rest of the water year.

Read aloud: Why is this catchment being observed?

About the observatory. Why is this catchment being observed?. Why Boscastle. Flash floods are increasing with climate change. Boscastle is a flashy catchment. Steep slopes and slow draining geology cause rapid response, even from small, localised events. How it is observed. We use innovative remote-sensing techniques (LiDAR, ultrasonic altimeter, camera) to monitor flash floods in real time, capturing accurate data on their speed and intensity. Impact. Enhance early warning systems and flood mitigation strategies to better protect communities from flash floods. While the case study focuses on Boscastle, UK, the methodology is applicable to other locations.
