## Design Context

### Users

Public visitors, flood researchers, project partners, and the professor reviewing the observatory. They may arrive to check current River Valency conditions, understand recent rainfall and river response, or inspect longer-term scientific context. The interface should support quick situational reading first and deeper exploration second.

### Brand Personality

Authoritative, field-scientific, editorial. The site should feel trustworthy and calm, like a well-produced field report from an active observatory rather than a generic technology dashboard. It should communicate confidence without overstating the provisional analysis.

### Aesthetic Direction

A cool, high-contrast public instrument designed to read clearly on an outdoor phone or a seminar-room screen. Use deep observatory navy for orientation, current-reading cobalt for emphasis, rainfall blue and river teal for data, crisp neutral canvases, and monospaced numerals within a single sans-serif system. Avoid generic card grids, decorative grids or glass effects, cream-toned editorial pastiche, and exaggerated emergency aesthetics outside genuine official warning states.

### Design Principles

1. Lead with present conditions: current river level, update freshness, and official warning context must be immediately legible.
2. Preserve scientific provenance: labels, units, time windows, and payload-provided explanatory copy remain visible and precise.
3. Reveal depth progressively: live observations come before event and historical analysis.
4. Use restraint for trust: colour and emphasis indicate meaning rather than decoration.
5. Design for public access: strong contrast, keyboard operation, useful empty states, reduced-motion support, and responsive layouts are required.
6. Preserve professor-approved semantics: exact scientific labels, shared chart windows, payload-driven copy, and supplementary official warning context take precedence over decorative reinterpretation.

## V2 Design Brief

### Feature Summary

`/v2/` is an isolated redesign prototype of the Flash Flood Observatory dashboard for public visitors and research collaborators. It consumes the existing public payload and provides a clearer, data-first route to understanding current conditions and scientific context without replacing or altering the production homepage.

### Primary User Action

Understand the current River Valency condition, then move naturally into recent rainfall, river response, and historical context.

### Design Direction

Express the authoritative, field-scientific context through a bright analytical canvas, a dark navigational frame, strong typographic hierarchy, disciplined hydrological colour, and visible data provenance. The prototype should feel like a live public instrument rather than an administrative dashboard or a static institutional report.

### Layout Strategy

Keep the V2 visual system in the masthead and hero, but reuse the production homepage's hero text fields without introducing a separate marketing headline: observatory name, location, payload strapline, freshness metadata, and current reading. Then place the five metrics in a high-density reading ledger. Use asymmetric space for the rainfall/depth observations and Event Analysis/historical range pairs so the layout reflects information priority. Retain the professor-approved order by placing the supplementary official Environment Agency warning section after the three project notes, followed by partner provenance.

### Key States

- Live: current values, update time, official alert state, and populated charts.
- Dry rainfall window: visible zero-valued chart with a minimum 1 mm axis.
- Feed unavailable: panel remains visible with a useful payload-provided message.
- Rating curve unavailable: Event Analysis explains what is required.
- Historical data unavailable: scatter and heatmap retain explanatory empty states.
- Official warning: alert styling becomes appropriately prominent without implying an observatory-generated warning.
- Loading/error: page structure remains stable and explains what could not be loaded.
- Mobile: essential status and metrics precede charts; controls remain reachable and charts scroll or resize without hiding information.

### Interaction Model

The shared 24-hour/5-day control updates all operational charts immediately. The heatmap year selector changes only the historical heatmap; its week selector navigates and highlights the daily grid without aggregating it. Each daily cell supports hover, tap, and roving keyboard focus, with a persistent readout for the selected day. Section navigation uses visible anchors. Primary controls support keyboard focus and touch targets of at least 44 pixels.

### Content Requirements

All public scientific content comes from `public/data/site_payload.json`. Static interface copy is limited to navigation and concise section introductions. Do not expose the page's prototype status in visible or accessibility labels. Error and empty-state copy states what happened and what remains available.

The public copy committed with the prototype was selected by the professor and is locked. Visual redesign work may reposition or restyle it, but must not rewrite, remove, or add visible wording. Proposed copy changes belong in a separate review note and should only be implemented after explicit professor approval.
