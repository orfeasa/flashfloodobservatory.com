## Design Context

### Users

Public visitors, flood researchers, project partners, and the professor reviewing the observatory. They may arrive to check current River Valency conditions, understand recent rainfall and river response, or inspect longer-term scientific context. The interface should support quick situational reading first and deeper exploration second.

### Brand Personality

Authoritative, field-scientific, editorial. The site should feel trustworthy and calm, like a well-produced field report from an active observatory rather than a generic technology dashboard. It should communicate confidence without overstating the provisional analysis.

### Aesthetic Direction

A light, tactile interface inspired by hydrology field notes, cartographic publications, and institutional research reports. Use warm paper-toned surfaces, ink-like text, restrained river green and mineral blue, precise rules, and generous editorial spacing. Avoid neon-on-dark technology styling, generic card grids, decorative glass effects, and exaggerated emergency aesthetics outside genuine official warning states.

### Design Principles

1. Lead with present conditions: current river level, update freshness, and official warning context must be immediately legible.
2. Preserve scientific provenance: labels, units, time windows, and payload-provided explanatory copy remain visible and precise.
3. Reveal depth progressively: live observations come before event and historical analysis.
4. Use restraint for trust: colour and emphasis indicate meaning rather than decoration.
5. Design for public access: strong contrast, keyboard operation, useful empty states, reduced-motion support, and responsive layouts are required.
6. Preserve professor-approved semantics: exact scientific labels, shared chart windows, payload-driven copy, and supplementary official warning context take precedence over decorative reinterpretation.

## V2 Design Brief

### Feature Summary

`/v2/` is an isolated redesign prototype of the Flash Flood Observatory dashboard for public visitors and research collaborators. It consumes the existing public payload and provides a calmer, more editorial route to understanding current conditions and scientific context without replacing or altering the production homepage.

### Primary User Action

Understand the current River Valency condition, then move naturally into recent rainfall, river response, and historical context.

### Design Direction

Express the authoritative, field-scientific, editorial context through a light report-like canvas, strong typographic hierarchy, measured natural colours, and visible data provenance. The prototype should feel like an active observatory publication rather than an administrative dashboard.

### Layout Strategy

Start with a compact masthead and explicit prototype label. Follow with a live-condition lead containing freshness and the current level. Place the five metrics in a horizontal field-note strip, then the shared time control and primary rainfall/depth observations. Event and historical analysis follow in a quieter section. Retain the professor-approved order by placing the supplementary official Environment Agency warning section after the three project notes, followed by partner provenance.

### Key States

- Live: current values, update time, official alert state, and populated charts.
- Dry rainfall window: visible zero-valued chart with a minimum 1 mm axis.
- Feed unavailable: panel remains visible with a useful payload-provided message.
- Rating curve unavailable: Event Analysis explains what is required.
- Historical data unavailable: scatter and heatmap retain explanatory empty states.
- Official warning: alert styling becomes appropriately prominent without implying an observatory-generated warning.
- Loading/error: page structure remains stable and explains what could not be loaded.
- Mobile: essential status and metrics precede charts; controls remain reachable and charts scroll or resize without hiding information.

### Interaction Model

The shared 24-hour/5-day control updates all operational charts immediately. The heatmap year selector changes only the historical heatmap. Section navigation uses visible anchors. All controls support keyboard focus and touch targets of at least 44 pixels.

### Content Requirements

All public scientific content comes from `public/data/site_payload.json`. Static prototype copy is limited to navigation, the prototype marker, and concise section introductions. Error and empty-state copy states what happened and what remains available.
